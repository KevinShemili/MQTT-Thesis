Methodological note: latency is reported as mean ± {{ConfidenceLevel}} confidence interval across {{RunCount}} independent Go benchmark runs, converted from Go's ns/op to µs for readability. The confidence interval uses Student's t with df={{DegreesOfFreedom}}. Wire overhead is the fixed number of bytes each scheme adds beyond the raw payload — nonce and AEAD tag for PSK, plus the encrypted session-key material for RSA and CP-ABE — measured once per case outside the timed loop. It is a property of the published envelope, not of the direction, so the decrypt tables reuse the overhead measured on the matching encrypt cases. The largest payload sizes exceed realistic MQTT message sizes and are included as a synthetic probe of asymptotic behavior.

Latency vs payload size

Lower values indicate faster encryption or decryption. Error bars show the confidence interval. At smaller payload sizes, RSA and CP-ABE are dominated by their fixed per-message session-key setup and asymmetric cryptographic work. As payload size increases, AES-GCM processing contributes a larger share of total latency, causing the RSA and PSK curves to move closer together. CP-ABE remains dominated by its asymmetric operation across the tested payload sizes.

Encrypt vs decrypt asymmetry

Which side of the pipeline each scheme punishes, at the smallest payload so the asymmetric cost is isolated. RSA is cheap to encrypt but expensive to decrypt, penalizing constrained subscribers; CP-ABE is the opposite, with encryption several times more expensive than decryption, penalizing constrained publishers. The ratio is stated explicitly above each pair, since the logarithmic axis compresses visual bar-height differences. PSK is near-symmetric and orders of magnitude below both.
